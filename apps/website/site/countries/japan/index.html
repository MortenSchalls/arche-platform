Country guide · Japan

Private flights to and from Japan

Every private or charter flight to Japan needs the authority's permission first. What the rules are, how far ahead to plan, and what they mean for your trip.

Permits for every flight

Permits are issued by the Civil Aviation Bureau, Ministry of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism (MLIT); applications go to its International Aviation Division1. The basic rule: a foreign aircraft flying to or from Japan needs an Article 126 permit, applied for 10 days before the flight1.

A charter with paying passengers needs a second permit: carrying paying passengers to or from Japan needs an Article 130-2 permit: 30 days ahead, 10 days if the operator has an office or agent in Japan, and 3 days (72 hours) for business, medical or tourism trips with an office or agent in Japan1. This is why the operator's presence in Japan, through an office or an agent, makes a large difference to how short the notice can be.

Domestic legs

Flights between two Japanese airports are tightly controlled: domestic legs need an Article 127 permit (free carriage, 3 days ahead); passengers may not board for a domestic leg only1. If your trip includes a domestic leg, we check with the operator before you see a quote. Our cabotage guide explains the general rule.

Airports and overflight

Not every airport can take a foreign aircraft: landing at an airport not designated by the Minister needs a separate permit1. Crossing Japan without landing: crossing Japanese airspace needs the same permit unless the aircraft flies only on airways1. One more condition: chartered capacity may not be resold; flying without a permit can mean prison or a fine1. Our Tokyo guide compares Haneda and Narita.

Who applies for the permit

The operator that flies your trip applies, usually through its handling agent or a flight-permit service. You do not need to deal with the authority yourself. What you can do is give us final names, passport details and times early, because a permit is issued for a given aircraft, schedule and passenger list, and late changes can mean a new application.

How we plan a private flight to or from Japan

Tell us where you are flying from or to, when, and how many people are travelling. We find aircraft from licensed operators, check each operator against our safety standard before you see a quote, and ask the operator to apply for the permits early, because the authority's answer decides the schedule. Permit rules change. The operator and its handling agent confirm the current rules for your flight before departure; every fact on this page shows its official source and the date we checked it.

Questions we are asked

How far ahead must a charter flight to Japan be arranged?

The rule for paying passengers is: carrying paying passengers to or from Japan needs an Article 130-2 permit: 30 days ahead, 10 days if the operator has an office or agent in Japan, and 3 days (72 hours) for business, medical or tourism trips with an office or agent in Japan1. We ask the operator to apply as soon as your plans are firm.

Does Archē Executive operate the flight?

No. We are a broker. A licensed operator flies every trip, and it is named in your quote and charter agreement before you confirm.
