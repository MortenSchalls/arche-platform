How we calculate

Distance is measured between the main airports of the two places along an approximate route that avoids airspace closed to aircraft registered in the EU, the UK, the US and Switzerland, and the conflict zones where the European Union Aviation Safety Agency's bulletins advise operators not to fly (checked 10 November 2026). An aircraft is shown as nonstop when that distance is within its published range: the maker's figure, flown in standard conditions with the reserves and load the maker states, which a full cabin, headwinds or a demanding airport can reduce. Otherwise it needs a fuel stop at a named airport (none in the Gulf while its bulletins apply), with each leg within 96% of the published range. Only aircraft that need no more than one fuel stop are shown. Flight time is the distance at the published cruise speed plus 24 minutes, and 1 hour 18 minutes for a fuel stop, in still air. The suggested aircraft needs the fewest stops and has the largest cabin (height by width) among the aircraft of the right size for the trip: light jets to super-midsize up to about three hours, super-midsize and heavy jets up to about six, heavy and ultra-long-range jets beyond. Each aircraft is shown by the variant its figures belong to. These are estimates for planning: the operator confirms the aircraft, the route and the time for your trip.
