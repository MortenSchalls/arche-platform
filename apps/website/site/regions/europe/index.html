Private flights in Europe

Europe has many airports used by private jets, with strict local rules on hours, noise and permission. What to know before you plan a trip, and our city and airport guides.

Draft · waiting for approval

Hours and night rules differ by airport

In Europe, the biggest difference between airports is often the opening hours. Farnborough near London is open 07:00 to 22:00 on weekdays and 08:00 to 20:00 at weekends and on bank holidays; closed on Christmas Day and Boxing Day1. At Paris–Le Bourget, jet take-offs, use of reverse thrust and use of runway 2 by aircraft over 5.7 tonnes are banned between 22:15 and 06:002. At Geneva, any planned commercial take-off or landing between 22:00 and 06:00 needs the airport operator's prior approval3.

Flights within the European Union

Inside the European Union, flights between EU airports are intra-Community air services, and Community air carriers shall be entitled to operate intra-Community air services4 (Article 15(1) of Regulation (EC) No 1008/20084). In practice, an operator from outside the EU cannot normally operate a flight between two EU airports. We check the operator's licence against every leg before you see a quote. Our cabotage guide explains the rules.

Permission and slots

Many European airports manage private flights through slots or permission. At Geneva, prior permission (PPR) needed for business and general aviation; bookings open 5 days ahead, made through the handling agent5. Nice is fully coordinated (Level 3): all operations need a slot; business and general aviation slots are requested by the handling agent from the French slot coordinator (COHOR)6. Others are simpler: at Biggin Hill near London, no runway slots required7, and Paris–Le Bourget is an IATA Level 1 airport8.
