Private flights in Asia-Pacific

Archē Executive is based in Singapore, and Asia-Pacific is our home region. Its airports have very different rules for private jets. What to know, and our city and airport guides.

Approvals before you fly

Several airports in the region need approval before a private flight. In Hong Kong, the operator needs a permit from the Director-General of Civil Aviation, applied for at least 3 working days before the flight, or at least 2 weeks ahead for a new or infrequent operator1. In Phuket, private jets need permission from the Civil Aviation Authority of Thailand and a ground handling agent, and the airport must be told at least 3 workdays before arrival2. At Tokyo Narita, business jets need a slot3. The operator applies; we make sure it has the passenger and trip details in good time.

Curfews and airport limits

Night curfews shape many trips. Singapore's business aviation airport, Seletar, has no flights between 22:00 and 07:00 Singapore time (14:00–23:00 UTC), except medical evacuation and emergency flights4. Sydney has a curfew from 11 pm to 6 am; only listed quieter aircraft, including approved business jets under 34,000 kg, may operate, taking off to the south and landing to the north over Botany Bay5. Narita has no take-offs or landings from midnight to 06:00 on runway A and from 23:00 to 06:00 on runway B, except in emergencies; a short flexible extension applies only for delays the airline could not avoid6. And some airline hubs limit private flights: Singapore Changi is not available to non-scheduled civil aircraft of 40 seats or fewer except in special circumstances7.

Domestic legs

Many trips in the region include a domestic leg, such as Bangkok to Phuket. Internationally, most countries prohibit cabotage for hire, and some also bar carrying non-paying passengers between two of their own airports8. We check the operator's permission for every domestic leg before you see a quote. Our cabotage guide explains the rules.
