Private flights to and from Tokyo

Tokyo's two international airports both take private jets, through dedicated business aviation facilities. How they compare, from the Japanese transport ministry and the airport operators.

Draft · waiting for approval

Haneda

Haneda is a state airport run by the Minister of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism1, in Ota City, Tokyo1, and it is open 24 hours2. For private flights, business aviation uses dedicated arrival and departure gates with their own customs and immigration3, and business aviation is not available at Terminal 13. It has four runways; the longest, C, is 3,360 m long and 60 m wide1.

Haneda is close to central Tokyo, which makes it the first choice for many trips.

Narita

Narita is run by Narita International Airport Corporation4. For private flights there is Premier Gate, an exclusive terminal for business jets with its own customs and immigration5. It has runway A, 4,000 m long and 60 m wide; runway B is currently 2,500 m by 60 m4.

Narita has a night curfew: no take-offs or landings from midnight to 06:00 on runway A and from 23:00 to 06:00 on runway B, except in emergencies; a short flexible extension applies only for delays the airline could not avoid6. And business jets need a slot7. The operator plans the flight around both.

Choosing the airport

For a daytime trip into central Tokyo, Haneda is usually the first choice if capacity is available. Narita is the alternative, outside its curfew. We check both for your aircraft and times before you see a quote.
