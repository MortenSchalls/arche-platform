How to read an operator's safety credentials

Every charter flight is flown by an operator, not by the broker. This guide explains the licence every operator must hold and the independent audits that go further, based on what the bodies that issue them publish.

Draft · waiting for approval

The licence: the Air Operator Certificate

The starting point is the operator's licence. As the Civil Aviation Authority of Singapore explains it, an Air Operator Certificate (AOC) is the authority's safety approval that lets an operator carry out specified commercial air transport flights1. For a Singapore operator the rule is simple: in Singapore an operator must hold a Singapore AOC to carry out commercial air transport1. The certificate is not a one-off: the regulator keeps inspecting AOC holders after the certificate is issued1.

Other countries work the same way, each through its own authority. The certificate comes with operations specifications that list what the operator may do and with which aircraft, so we check that the aircraft offered for your trip is covered. The US regulator gives private customers the same advice we follow ourselves: the US FAA tells charter customers to ask to see the operator's certificate2.

Independent audits: going beyond the licence

A licence shows that the authority has approved the operator. Independent audits look at how the operator actually runs its safety management, and they are voluntary. Three are widely used in business aviation.

ARGUS: ARGUS International, founded in 1995, provides specialised aviation audit services3. It rates operators at several levels. An ARGUS Gold rating uses a remote audit to check the operator's safety management system and maintenance practices4; ARGUS Gold Certified adds an on-site audit to the Gold checks4. At the next level, an ARGUS Platinum rating rests on a two-day on-site audit of the operator's safety management system5, and a Platinum operator must pass a renewal audit every two years5. The top level is Platinum Elite: ARGUS Platinum Elite requires at least two Platinum audits in a row with no significant findings6, and Platinum Elite operators have maintenance and operations reviews every six months6.

WYVERN: the WYVERN Wingman Standard, published by WYVERN Ltd, sets safety performance expectations for aircraft operators and is voluntary7. Wingman requires a safety management system that meets FAA/CAA rules or ICAO Annex 197. Wingman audits traditionally run on a 24-month cycle7, and Wingman operators go through an on-site audit of two to three days8.

IS-BAO: IS-BAO is the International Standard for Business Aircraft Operations, run by IBAC9. IBAC describes IS-BAO as the only ICAO-recognised industry standards programme9. Registration has stages: IS-BAO Stage 1 confirms the safety management system is in place9, while IS-BAO Stage 3 confirms safety management is fully built into the operator's business9. IS-BAO registration needs an independent third-party audit10, and IS-BAO registration is renewed every two or three years10.

What we check before you see a quote

Before we propose an operator we check its certificate and the aircraft listed on it, its insurance, and its audit status with the audit bodies above. Where an operator has no independent audit, we tell you before you decide.

Audit claims must be accurate. Under US rules, US rules forbid a broker to misstate that an operator meets an auditing organisation's standard11. We hold ourselves to the same standard everywhere: we only state an audit result we have checked with the audit body.

Questions to ask any broker

Who is the operator, and may I see its certificate? Is this aircraft on that certificate? Which independent audit does the operator hold, and when does it expire? A good broker answers all three in writing before you sign.

Questions we are asked

Is an audited operator safer than one without an audit?

An audit is an extra, independent check of how the operator manages safety. It does not replace the authority's licence, and no audit is a promise about a single flight. It is one of several things we weigh before proposing an operator.

Does Archē Executive operate aircraft?

No. We are a broker. Every flight is operated by a licensed operator, named in your quote and your charter agreement before you confirm.
