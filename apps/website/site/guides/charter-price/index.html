What makes up a charter price

A charter price is not a ticket price. It is the cost of the whole aircraft for your trip, built from the operator's price, airport charges and taxes. This guide explains each part, with the official sources for the taxes.

The operator's price

Most of the price is the operator's charge for the aircraft and crew. It depends on the flying time for your trip, the aircraft type and any positioning the aircraft needs to reach you and return. Waiting days away from base, crew overnight costs and catering are added where they apply.

Because we are a broker, we compare operators for each trip and add our service fee to the operator's price. You see one total price before you confirm.

Airport charges

Every airport publishes its own charges, and the operator passes them on. The published list for Singapore Changi shows how they are built: at Singapore Changi, landing charges are based on the aircraft's maximum taxi weight1. Changi charges aircraft parking per 24 hours, with the first 3 hours free1. Changi also lists passenger charges: a service and security fee, an aviation levy and an airport development levy1. Handling is charged separately by the handling agent the operator appoints.

Taxes

Taxes depend on where the flight starts and ends. In the United States, US federal law taxes amounts paid for taxable air transport of persons at 7.5 percent2. US law also charges a tax on each domestic flight segment2, and for a chartered aircraft the IRS works out the segment tax by multiplying it by the number of passengers on board3. A separate US tax applies to international air transport that begins or ends in the United States3.

In the United Kingdom, UK Air Passenger Duty's higher rate applies to aircraft of 20 tonnes or more with fewer than 19 seats4. The current UK APD rates apply to passengers carried on or after 1 April 20264.

In Singapore, international transport of passengers can be charged GST at 0% (zero-rated)5; this covers flights from Singapore abroad, from abroad to Singapore, and between two places outside Singapore5.

What you should always be told

US rules for charter brokers set a clear standard: US rules make a broker disclose the total cost paid by the charterer to or through the broker6, and they also make the broker disclose fees collected by third parties, such as fuel, landing and parking or hangar fees7. We apply the same standard everywhere. Your quote shows the total price, what is included, and any items charged afterwards at cost, such as de-icing, with the supplier's receipts.

We do not publish prices on this website: every trip is priced for its own aircraft, route and dates. Tell us the trip and you receive a written quote with the operator named.

Questions we are asked

Why do two quotes for the same trip differ?

Different aircraft, positioning distances, crew costs and airport charges. We show you what each quote includes, so you compare like with like.

Are taxes included in the price?

Your quote states which taxes and charges are included and which, if any, are charged afterwards at cost.
